AI + Research

1. ChatGPT and Codex are one now

No need for me to push you to jump from ChatGPT to Codex. They are one now. The important question is not which interface we start from, but what the task requires next.

The same workflow can move between conversation, research, coding, files, testing, and interpretation. ChatGPT and Codex are different ways of working with the same kind of computational collaborator.

Domain knowledge

Provide the scientific context and decide what matters.

Logical thinking

Break down the problem, test assumptions, and notice contradictions.

Self-directed initiative

The drive to notice what needs to be done, start before being asked, keep asking, testing, revising, and pushing until the work becomes real.

My takeaway: The question is no longer, "Should I use ChatGPT or Codex?" The better question is, "What does this task require next?" AI raises the return on the user's own ability, but the researcher still defines the problem and checks the result.
Learning by building: I do not regret setting up OpenClaw. Building it taught me how agents use context, tools, permissions, workflows, and debugging. Now ChatGPT and Codex bring many of those capabilities together, but the understanding came from doing the work.

Comparative Genomics

4. Farm animal evolution reveals hidden regulation in human traits

Most human GWAS variants are non-coding and are thought to influence traits by changing gene expression. Yet researchers often cannot connect these variants to eQTLs, creating a "missing regulation" problem.

Human data

About 15%
of GWAS heritability mediated by eQTLs in the study's comparison.
About 42%
of GWAS associations explained by eQTL colocalization.

Cattle and pigs

More than 50%
of GWAS heritability mediated by eQTLs.
More than 85%
of cattle GWAS associations explained; pigs reached 55%.

The authors argue that domestication and selective breeding created strong population bottlenecks. These changed allele frequencies and made some regulatory variants easier to detect than they are in human data.

My takeaway: Human genetic data show only the part of biology that our evolutionary history, allele frequencies, sample sizes, and experimental designs allow us to see. Comparative genomics can act like a natural experiment: other species may reveal regulatory mechanisms that are too rare or too context-specific to detect in humans.

Reflection + Growth

5. Do not underestimate yourself because progress is slow

The curve in this video illustrates why progress can be difficult to see at first. Small improvements may look insignificant for a long time, but consistent effort can eventually create a large difference.

Curve-only version of the source video.
My takeaway: We often judge ourselves by visible short-term results. That can be misleading. Research skills, technical ability, judgment, and confidence develop quietly before the results become obvious. Do not mistake slow progress for limited ability. A slow phase may be the period in which the foundation is being built.

Summary

Key takeaways

  • ChatGPT and Codex are increasingly one workflow; choose the next action, not a brand.
  • AI is most useful when the researcher brings domain knowledge, logic, and initiative.
  • The value of a biomedical agent comes from its environment of tools, data, code, and feedback.
  • Building a system is also a way to learn how systems work, even when the tool changes.
  • Other species can reveal human biology that our own data do not yet make visible.
  • Slow progress is not the same as no progress.
