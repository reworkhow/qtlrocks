1 / Statistics in practice

A Significant Result Is Not Always a Strong Result

A recent Science paper analyzed 2,344 Chinese counties and reported that a one-standard-deviation increase in the Photovoltaic Policy Stringency Index (PSI), a measure of how strongly local policies promoted photovoltaic expansion, was associated with a 2.10% decrease in bird diversity. Its title states the conclusion directly: China's solar expansion policy reduces bird diversity.

Original estimate
-2.10%
Reported change in the Shannon index per one-standard-deviation increase in policy stringency.
Published commentary
Within R2 = 0.0007
PSI-only model: 1.8% of the full model's within-model explanatory variation.
Preprint reanalysis
+0.58%
After adding observer count: P = 0.288, no longer statistically significant.

What the critics argue

The reported overall R2 is dominated by fixed effects, while PSI explains very little remaining within-county variation. A separate reanalysis argues that changing citizen-science observation effort can alter the estimated effect; its instrumental-variable estimate also becomes nonsignificant (P = 0.912).

What the authors respond

Observation duration and policy effects are not directly comparable. Policy variables can explain a small share of environmental variation and still matter because policy is one of the factors people can change.

My takeaway: Even a paper in Science can have statistical analysis that is debatable or only moderately convincing.

A prestigious journal does not remove the need to distinguish statistical significance, effect size, explanatory importance, robustness, and evidence for causality. Read beyond the title and inspect the variables, model, data-generating process, and sensitivity analyses.

The classic chocolate-Nobel correlation makes the same point: a plausible association can still be driven by a third factor such as national wealth rather than the proposed causal story.

GPT-6 Astra Is Here

OpenAI announced GPT-6 Astra on September 3, 2026. It appears as GPT-6 Pro in ChatGPT. It is rolling out first through the Trusted Access Program, with availability through the API and eligible Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans expanding over the following days.

Astra is designed for complex reasoning, coding, research, computer use, and document creation. The meaningful change is its ability to carry more of a workflow across tools, rather than merely answer isolated questions.

My takeaway: Better AI makes human judgment more important, not less.

As AI takes on larger parts of a workflow, our value shifts toward choosing the right problem, providing domain knowledge, defining success clearly, setting boundaries, and verifying whether the result is correct.

3 / Research practice

My Four-Step Workflow for Reading Scientific Papers (Without Trying to Read Them All)

Scientific papers should not all receive the same amount of attention. Shea Andrews describes reading as a sequence of decisions that progressively narrows the literature while increasing the attention given to each paper.

Stage 01
Discovery
Find papers through alerts, colleagues, searches, and AI tools.
Is this potentially relevant?
Stage 02
Triage
Use titles and abstracts to filter the incoming literature.
Ignore, save, or continue?
Stage 03
Skimming
Identify the question, assumptions, and main findings.
Do I need deeper understanding?
Stage 04
Deep reading
Interrogate the design, methods, evidence, and interpretation.
Can I explain and evaluate it?
More papers More attention per paper

AI can assist with discovery, triage, and targeted extraction. Its summaries and citations still need to be checked against the original paper.

Beyond the to-read list: A reference manager should be a searchable map of the literature, not an ever-growing obligation.

The goal is to decide deliberately whether to ignore a paper, save it, skim it for context, or read it deeply because your own experiment, analysis, grant, or scientific claim depends on it.

4 / AI adoption

AI Lowers Activation Energy, but It Cannot Create Motivation

Khan Academy built Khanmigo as an always-available AI tutor. Sal Khan describes the role of educational technology using a chemistry analogy:

"It will lower the activation energy to learn. It'll make it easier to learn."
Without AI support With AI support Human motivation and support still provide the push.

This newsletter is a simple example. I had wanted to make one for a long time, but it required too much effort to produce consistently. With AI, I can now turn my ideas into a finished newsletter in about 30 minutes. By lowering the barrier, AI makes me both more willing and happier to do it regularly.

Lowering the barrier is not the same as getting people across it. Khan later described Khanmigo as a "non-event" for many students because they did not use it much. A reactive tutor cannot help someone who does not seek assistance, does not recognize the gap, or does not know how to formulate a useful question.

My takeaway: AI can reduce friction, but it cannot replace self-directed initiative.

A powerful assistant creates an opportunity. Someone still has to recognize the opportunity, begin the interaction, ask questions, and continue when the work becomes difficult. Access to AI and effective use of AI are two different things.
