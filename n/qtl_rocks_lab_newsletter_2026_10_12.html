1 / Experiencing AI

The LLM Capability Gap Is Becoming a Funnel

Andrej Karpathy describes a widening gap in how people perceive AI. Casual chatbot users and people using agents on complex technical projects can have very different experiences. He sketches four groups, shaped by the problems people attempt, the domain, and their access to models and tools.

My takeaway: Form your view through direct experience. Try AI on a substantial research task with results you can check, and learn where it helps and where it fails.

2 / Research and careers

“They Have Annihilated My Field of Research”

Hugo Duminil-Copin, a 2022 Fields Medal winner, said this at an October 8 meeting of the French Mathematical Society. Le Monde reports that OpenAI's release touched at least 16 questions he cared about, with one research programme spanning 13 manuscripts. He described crying after the announcement and expressed concern for young researchers.

OpenAI released mathematical work produced by an unreleased internal model on October 6. Its catalogue, checked October 11, reports:

719ManuscriptsAcross multiple disciplines
372Result familiesGroups of related papers
~42%Top-line results formalizedIn Lean, for computer checking

Verification remains ongoing. These figures do not represent 719 independently validated breakthroughs.

What is our advantage now?

AI can increasingly help choose questions, assess novelty, check analyses, and develop biological hypotheses. We cannot assume these will remain exclusively human strengths. Our practical advantage is combining domain knowledge, detailed project context, distinctive data, experiments, and trusted collaborations with AI.

The timing and extent of job losses remain uncertain. Automating tasks does not establish that an entire role will disappear, but changing capabilities can reshape research agendas and expectations.

My takeaway: Take the change seriously. Build our ability to conduct reliable research with AI, while developing the expertise needed to challenge its answers. Our advantage needs to keep developing.

3 / Tools to try

REA: From an Executable to a Working Reconstruction

Imagine giving your agent a program and asking: “Understand how this works, then build a version I can modify.”

REA (Reverse Engineer Anything) connects coding agents to software-inspection tools. Agents can investigate compiled instructions and application behavior, reconstruct algorithms, and write and test new implementations. REA supplies the inspection tools; the reconstruction projects supply code and reference checks.

Published reconstruction projects

TH095 / Shoot the Bullet

The maintainer reports reconstructed source for all identified game functions and a playable Windows build about 12 days after initialization.

Development account

These are project-reported milestones, not a general benchmark or a guarantee that any application can be rebuilt.

A question for students

Could an AI agent using REA rebuild BLUPF90 from its executables and documentation? What would convince you that the reconstruction works?

My answer: Reproducing a substantial subset seems plausible. Published methods provide the mathematical foundation; REA could help investigate implementation details. A convincing result needs comparisons of numerical outputs, convergence, runtime, and memory across datasets and models. Replacing the mature suite requires much broader validation. This is an experiment we could investigate, not an achievement we have demonstrated.

My takeaway: Pick something you are curious about and try it. A small program or feature you can inspect and test gives your agent a clear goal and a way to compare its reconstruction with the original.

4 / Terence Tao's talk

Math 2.0: From Proof Scarcity to Proof Abundance

In his October 9 Caltech lecture, Terence Tao describes mathematics moving from proof scarcity to proof abundance. He identifies three reasons mathematical problems are particularly suitable for modern AI:

“This combination of objective verifiability, digitizability, and large, high-quality data sets have made the task of solving mathematical problems particularly amenable to the machine learning techniques that power modern AI.”

Terence Tao, Math 2.0
Math 1.0Proof scarcity

Finding a proof is difficult and rare.

→
Math 2.0Proof abundance

AI changes what can be generated and checked.

Tao argues that maximizing completed solutions can miss deeper goals: developing methods, understanding, and new directions. His vision of Math 2.0 includes:

Explain opaque proofs

Find clearer explanations for results we already know.

Connect mathematical knowledge

Organize research papers into living networks of results.

Explore assumptions

Investigate what survives when hypotheses are changed or removed.

Build reusable foundations

Create formal libraries and identify new principles and methods.

He also calls for transparent assessments that report failures and computational costs alongside successes.

My takeaway: An answer can be valuable; understanding its connections creates further value. Use AI to explore, explain, check, and build on results. Tao closes by emphasizing imagination in how we use these tools.
