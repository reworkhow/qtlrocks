1 / Making With AI

The Little Prince: From a Story to a Playable World

What if you could walk through The Little Prince rather than just read it? The Little Prince: Among the Stars turns the story into a browser-based 3D adventure, with planets to explore and characters to encounter. Play the game.

Its creator, Sac, reports building it in 16 days, using Claude Opus 5.5 for coding, Tripo for 3D asset generation, and Blender for asset editing. It is a striking example of combining AI tools to turn a creative idea into something people can actually use.

My Take

Before AI, two people could look similar from the outside: neither had built anything. But the reasons could be very different. One had ideas and wanted to experiment, yet could not justify spending months learning the tools and implementing them. The other was not interested in starting.

AI lowers those barriers. Someone with curiosity, ideas, and self-directed initiative can now attempt projects that previously felt out of reach. This game illustrates that possibility: an idea that might have remained “something I would love to make someday” became something people can play.

The distinction is not simply between people who use AI and people who do not. It is between waiting for something to happen and taking the initiative to make it happen.

AI can reduce the activation energy needed to begin. But we still need the drive to notice possibilities, start, experiment, and keep improving.

As implementation becomes easier, what we choose to attempt matters more.

The development timeline and tool use are reported by the creator, not evidence that AI produced the entire game autonomously.

2 / Working With AI

How Employment Is Changing at Anthropic

Anthropic's October 16 report offers a glimpse of work inside an organization heavily using AI agents. Four findings stand out:

For the 6:1 comparison, employee hours are estimated from headcount and a 40-hour week; agent hours come from session telemetry.

Hiring assessments increasingly emphasized autonomy and judgment, relative to narrow technical expertise and implementation skills.

My Take

This is another angle on the lesson from The Little Prince.

Before AI, routine implementation could dominate everyone's work. Two students might both spend days writing and debugging an R script. Their progress could look similar, even if they differed considerably in their ability to identify useful questions, choose an approach, or interpret results.

When AI handles much of that implementation, those differences become more visible. What do you choose to investigate? Can you recognize when an analysis is going in the wrong direction? Do you initiate the next useful step, or wait for instructions?

The routine work has not disappeared completely, and technical understanding still matters. But completing a prescribed task is becoming less distinguishing than deciding what needs to be done and judging whether it was done well.

As routine execution becomes easier, differences in autonomy and judgment become more visible.
