1 / Statistical Genetics

Genomic Analyses Reveal New Insights into Alzheimer's Disease

Uffelmann et al. · medRxiv preprint · revised May 2026

Same GWAS data, different SNP heritability: approximately 6% with LD score regression (LDSC), versus 16% with SBayesRC in the revised preprint.

How Did They Validate the Estimate?

  1. Start with known truth. Using 348,494 UK Biobank genotypes, they simulated a 5%-prevalence disease with target liability heritability of 12%: 6% from APOE-ε4 and 6% from 1,000 other SNPs. Five replicates tested whether the estimators recovered that target.
  2. Stress-test the large-effect locus. The standard all-SNP random-effects model showed modest upward bias. Fitting the APOE tagging variant as a fixed effect and excluding correlated neighboring SNPs removed the reported bias in this simulation; LDSC remained downward-biased.
  3. Carry the correction back to real data. The adjusted SBayesRC estimate fell from approximately 18% to 16%, rather than retaining the larger estimate.
Additional Robustness Checks: Annotations, LD, and Convergence
  • Annotation-free simulation: they used SBayesR without annotations, because no annotation effects were simulated. The recovery check therefore did not depend on supplying functional annotation information.
  • Alternative LD handling: they tested exclusions based on LD with APOE (r2 > 0.001) or its entire LD block. They also tested LDSC with fuller LD-score coverage because the APOE tagging variant was absent from the HapMap3 LD-score files.
  • Numerical stability: four independent MCMC chains and a convergence diagnostic assessed whether sampling had stabilized. Convergence is a computational check, not proof that the model is scientifically correct.

See the revised paper's Methods and Supplementary Data 18. Five replicates under an Alzheimer's-like architecture support this specific setting, not universal robustness across traits.

A complementary leave-one-cohort-out prediction check achieved an average incremental liability-scale R2 of 12.8% in European-ancestry cohorts. This tests out-of-sample prediction, not unbiased heritability estimation by itself.

My Take

Do not simply prefer the larger estimate. Construct a relevant simulation, check recovery of known truth, diagnose failures in your own preferred method, and then return to the real data. That is the validation story worth learning from.

Version matters: the original PMC abstract reports 19%; this item uses the revised estimate of 16%.

Leveraging Genomic Deep Learning Models for the Prediction of Noncoding Variant Effects

Kathail, Bajwa & Ioannidis · Nature Genetics · October 2026

This is a review, not a new model competing with AlphaGenome. It distinguishes models trained on measured molecular activity from DNA language models initially trained on sequence itself.

Lower sequence likelihood does not mean lower expression. Language models can also be fine-tuned on functional data; this illustrates zero-shot likelihood scoring only.

The benchmark matters: distinguishing functional from nonfunctional regions is easier than identifying which particular variant changes function within the same regulatory region.

My Take

Define the biological quantity before choosing the model. A predicted molecular effect can be a useful annotation, but is not automatically a causal effect on a complex trait or a SNP effect for genomic prediction.

3 / AI Meets Experiments

ChatGPT Meets the Robot Lab

Nobel laureate Omar Yaghi describes a researcher-directed workflow for improving a covalent organic framework's crystallinity: ChatGPT proposed 96 initial synthesis conditions, a robotic platform filled the reaction wells, and X-ray diffraction assessed the resulting materials.

  1. 01 / ChatGPTPropose conditions
  2. 02 / RoboticsPrepare experiments
  3. 03 / MeasurementAssess crystallinity
  4. 04 / FeedbackGuide the next round

Experimental results were returned to ChatGPT. After three cycles, Yaghi reports crystallinity approximately three times the literature benchmark, following a problem researchers had pursued for about a decade.

My Take

The important unit is the closed loop, not a chatbot answer: propose, test, measure, and revise. This is AI-assisted experimentation with human oversight, not ChatGPT independently conducting all the research.

4 / Genome Interpretation

Claude Analyzes a Genome in 30 Minutes

Stanford geneticist Euan Ashley gave Claude his decade-old variant call file and a roughly 180-word prompt. He reports recovering important known findings in about 30 minutes.

9 months / 30 peoplePioneering team effort in 2009
~30 minutesAshley's reported Claude self-test
  • Rare disease: no major Mendelian loss-of-function finding, consistent with the previous analysis.
  • Known risk: identified previously known APOE ε4 homozygosity, a specific finding rather than a genome-wide polygenic score.
  • Pharmacogenomics: flagged findings involving DPYD and CYP2C19.

It also recognized a limit: the old, non-gVCF file could not support reliable genome-wide polygenic risk scores.

Reference does not mean benign. Ashley reports that Claude recognized the problem of risk alleles appearing in hg19 itself. The actual alleles and reference build matter, not simply whether a sequence differs from the reference.

My Take

The historical effort analyzed Stephen Quake's genome and helped establish the interpretation framework. This is not a controlled comparison: Claude now draws on years of accumulated tools and knowledge. The opportunity is easier access to that infrastructure, while retaining the expertise to check answers and input limitations.

The plan includes commercially available frontier OpenAI models within plan usage limits, and GPT-Rosalind following trusted-access approval. GPT-Rosalind is designed for life sciences research, including genomics, experimental planning, and animal health. API access is not included.

My Take: Build Your Own Research Loop

We now have access to these tools. I would like each of you to build a small AI-assisted closed loop around a real question in your project:

Propose → Run → Check → Feed Back → Revise

Use AI to help develop an approach, run the analysis or experiment, and check the result against data, a strong baseline, or a problem with a known answer. Then feed those findings back into the next iteration.

Like Yaghi's example, the opportunity is to connect suggestions to measured results and repeated improvement. The loop does not need to be fully automated. Your domain knowledge and judgment are part of it.

Why Do We Divide Prediction Correlation by the Square Root of Heritability?

By Roddur

We often validate genomic predictions against phenotypes, but our target is the true breeding value. Roddur explains why the correlation with a noisy phenotype (predictive ability) differs from the correlation with the true breeding value (genetic prediction accuracy).
